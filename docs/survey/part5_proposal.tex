\section{What the evidence implies for the design}
\label{sec:design}

Everything above now compresses without loss. The failure record says the
defects are layered and the deep layers decide acceptance; the literature
says the shallow layers are measurable and the deep ones are only reachable
by editing and by readers; the tool field supplies the shallow measurements
nearly for free and supplies nothing for the deep layers. So \hv{} is a
two-layer design. A \emph{measurement layer} of deterministic diagnostics
runs constantly, reports numbers with locations, and gates nothing. An
\emph{acceptance layer} of context-starved reader proxies and, finally, the
human reader gates everything. Between them sits an editing workflow that
repairs in layer order under protected baselines.

Table~\ref{tab:requirements} states the requirements this survey commits the
design to, each with its evidence. They are numbered for reference from the
work packages; none is aspirational, in the sense that every one of them
traces to a failure we paid for or a result we verified.

One requirement deserves a paragraph rather than a row, because it is the
one a tooling project most easily forgets. The acceptance metric for \hv{}
itself is the number of human-feedback rounds a document needs before its
reader accepts it. The ZLB rescue took five. SMEwallet's units took one to
three each. The BGS volume never arrived. Every work package below either
reduces that number or exists to measure whether something else did; any
proposed activity that does neither is out of scope by construction, which
is the practical meaning of R10.

\section{The proposal}
\label{sec:proposal}

\subsection{Shape of the repository}

\hv{} consolidates into five trees, all under the existing repository.
\path{policies/} becomes the single home of the writing doctrine: the prose
policy's reader-facing sections, the two claudecodex skills, MathDevMCP's
repair skill, the installed humanizer list, and a new scholarly layer
merged from academic-humanizer's Layers 2--4 with examples transposed to
economics (R2 makes claudecodex the distributor, no longer a source of
truth). \path{diagnostics/} is the measurement layer, one CLI named
\texttt{hv}. \path{corpus/} holds the evaluation assets. \path{harness/}
holds reader-proxy templates and the behavioral tests. \path{vendor/}
stays as the pinned external tools with their manifest.

\subsection{The \texttt{hv} commands, specified}

Each command below lists what it consumes, what it emits, what it is built
from, and how we will know it works. All of them obey R3: numbers out,
no edits in.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv leak}} consumes rendered text (pdftotext output) plus
the manuscript source; emits counts and locations of internal-register
leakage: file paths, dates-as-scoping, identifiers, and a
project-configurable banned-vocabulary map seeded from the ZLB style
contract's ban list and the BGS audit's administrative-register examples.
Built as a small matcher over per-project YAML config. Acceptance: on the
ZLB pre-rescue markdown it must fire heavily; on the accepted final PDF
text it must report zero, reproducing the recorded leak-sweep result.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv rhythm}} consumes source and detexed text; emits the
mechanical-tell profile: dash counts, sentence-opener and
paragraph-opener distributions, section-shape clustering, transition-phrase
counts, sentence-length mean and coefficient of variation, paragraph
monotony, tricolon density. Built by orchestrating sloptrim and the curated
vale-ai-tells experimental metrics, with the four destructive rules
disabled for \texttt{.tex} (R2) and the detex path handling the artifacts
observed in Section~\ref{sec:oss-sloptrim}. Acceptance is historical
replay: on the preserved ZLB pass-two snapshot it must reproduce the
recorded numbers, 133 em dashes and 20 of 55 sections opening with ``The'';
on the final manuscript, 0 and at most 4 per opener word; on the three
calibration documents, sloptrim's 45/25/10 within tolerance.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv defensive}} consumes detexed text; emits candidate
sentences with surrounding context for the defensive-register classes:
negative definitions without mechanisms, prohibitions without consequences,
the moralizing tics, unraised-objection answers (humanizer patterns 34--35,
the DefensiveHedges shapes, and the BGS defensive-qualification examples as
seeds). Each candidate is emitted with the editorial question attached,
``does this sentence give the reader a reason, or only protect the
author?'', for an editing model or human to adjudicate (R7: flags, not
fixes). Acceptance: on the preserved ZLB pass-three text it must recover a
majority of the ~65 sentences the manual diagnostic found, and its
false-positive rate on the accepted final text must be low enough that an
editor reviews its output without resentment; both measured, with the
corpus, in WP3.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv diff}} consumes two versions of a manuscript; emits
the protected-baseline report: equation bodies byte-compared, label,
citation, and reference censuses, and an explain-every-removal list of
substantive deletions (R5). Built on LaTeX-aware extraction, piloted per
Section~\ref{sec:oss-latex}. Acceptance: replaying the five ZLB passes must
show 109 of 109 equations identical at every step and flag the deliberate
reference additions of the final pass and nothing else.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv gate}} consumes the manuscript; emits the compile
gate (N-pass build, unresolved references, overfull-box budget) and
rendered page images for the title, a math-dense page, and every table,
institutionalizing rendered-page inspection. Built from the repair skill's
two scripts (Section~\ref{sec:tool-repair}). Acceptance: reproduces the
recorded ZLB build-gate outcomes.

\paragraph{\texttt{hv drift}} consumes a corpus of our manuscripts plus the
references; emits quarterly population statistics: excess-vocabulary
profiles against the pre-2022 economics baseline
(eqs.~\eqref{eq:kobak-p}--\eqref{eq:kobak-q}, via adapted slop-forensics),
the mixture-estimated machine-typical fraction $\hat{\alpha}$ of
eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture} with $Q$ estimated from our own agents' raw drafts,
cross-document structural similarity (R12), and optionally an aggregate
curvature distribution. It has no per-document mode, by construction
(R11): the command refuses single-file input. Acceptance: on a synthetic
mixture of known composition, $\hat{\alpha}$ within the validation error
reported by \citet{liang2024monitoring}.

\subsection{Work packages}

\paragraph{WP1: consolidation.} Move the doctrine into \path{policies/},
merge the scholarly layer, convert the humanizer precedence header into
diagnostic configuration, add Claude-native skill installation beside the
Codex path, and leave claudecodex as pure distribution. Deliverable: one
install command produces both agent environments from the \hv{} source of
truth; the existing phrase-lock tests pass against the new layout.
Effort: small; risk: low; unblocked today.

\paragraph{WP2: the measurement layer.} Build the six commands above, in
the order leak, rhythm, diff, gate, defensive, drift, because the first
four are configuration and replay while the last two need corpus work.
Includes the LaTeX-host pilot (Vale-on-detex versus TeXtidote). The
acceptance tests are the historical replays listed per command; a tool
that cannot reproduce the recorded history does not ship. Unblocked
today; the replay fixtures (ZLB snapshots) already exist in the BayesFilter
repository.

\paragraph{WP3: corpus and behavioral evidence.} Assemble \path{corpus/}:
the 815 BGS repair pairs with their taxonomy labels, the ZLB pass
snapshots with their measured counts and final acceptance, the SMEwallet
draft-10/draft-11 units with recorded PI verdicts, and a pre-2022
economics reference corpus. Then run the experiment the stack has never
had (R9): matched rewrites of corpus units with and without each skill,
scored by \texttt{hv}, judged pairwise under the R8 protocol, the judge
itself validated against the recorded human verdicts. Deliverable: a
scorecard per skill stating what it measurably changes, plus the
convergence baseline for R12. Needs WP2's scorers.

\paragraph{WP4: the editorial workflow.} One skill codifying the pipeline
the record validated: two-document setup with identifier map (R1), a
drafting contract in the ZLB style-contract form, repair in layer order
(R4) with \texttt{hv} measurements before and after each pass, a single
voice editor after any parallel drafting, per-agent drop reports,
protected baselines via \texttt{hv diff} (R5), then low-context
reader-proxy review descended from the AIpostdoc reader-pull personas with
forced choices and the injection boundary, and the human read as the only
promotion (R8's proxy explicitly cannot promote). Mostly consolidation of
existing text; the work is deleting the ceremony from it (R10).

\paragraph{WP5: the pilot.} Apply the full pipeline to one live document,
the next BayesFilter companion note or one CIP monograph part, with the
ZLB rescue as benchmark: five feedback rounds to acceptance. The pilot's
single question is whether the toolkit reduces that count. Its result
note, one page, states the count, what consumed the rounds, and which
tools earned their place. Needs WP1, WP2, WP4; WP3 can run concurrently.

WP1 and WP2 start immediately and independently. Per R10, each package
carries one plan and one result note; a package that begins generating
review packets, milestone registries, or state files is failing by
definition, and the correct repair is deletion, not documentation.

\subsection{Risks, stated with their controls}

Four risks have known shapes, because we have watched each one happen.
The toolkit becomes the new template and the fleet converges on a
detectable house style: R12's convergence monitoring exists for this, and
R3 (report, never auto-fix) keeps a human or editing agent's judgment in
every repair. The gates ossify and get gamed, the BGS checkpoint story:
diagnostics here gate nothing, so there is nothing to game except the
human read, which cannot be gamed, only failed. The project spends itself
on instrumentation, the BGS process story: the WP budget is the one-page
plan-and-result cap, and the pilot metric is the only scoreboard. And the
drift instruments get misused for attribution: R11 firewalls them, the
drift command refuses per-document input, and if the discipline ever
slips, the instrument goes, not the firewall.

What this proposal does not claim, so that nobody reads more into it than
the evidence holds: no tool here, ours or adopted, has yet been shown to
reduce human-feedback rounds; that is exactly what WP3 and WP5 measure.
The literature's load-bearing results were verified at method level, the
supporting ones at abstract level, and StoryScope remains secondhand. The
sloptrim accuracy figures are its own benchmark. And the reader-proxy
reviews, however well they predicted one human rejection, are proxies:
the record contains one case of proxy-predicts-human, not a validation
study, which is another thing WP3's corpus makes testable.
